University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill

Over the summer of my junior year in high school, I volunteered at a local clinic in my hometown where I collected patient vitals and input patient data into the medical record charts. Through these responsibilities, I gained firsthand exposure to the day-to-day operations of a healthcare setting. However, outside of my clinical responsibilities, through my close interactions with patients, I saw how patients avoided care due to cost. I noticed that systemic disparities in healthcare posed a hinderance for healthcare access. This experience inspired me to deepen my understanding of broader patterns that contribute to unequal access to healthcare. Consequently, I became fascinated with public health. My interest in public health, specifically biostatistics, deepened through a project in my AP Calculus class, where I explored the real-world implications of mathematics. Through this, I was enlightened on the role of quantitative analysis in its relation to identifying disparities and forming public health solutions to improve healthcare. I now attend UNC Chapel Hill, where I am taking pre-requisites for biostatistics courses, as I prepare to apply to UNC Gillings School of Public Health as a Biostatistics major. Through this major, I intend to delve into the quantitative side of healthcare to address public health problems. Outside of my pre-health interest in addressing inequalities in healthcare, I currently serve as the Social Justice Advocate (SJA) for Craige’s Community Government to promote equality on campus and voice student concerns. I am also on the Residence Hall Association’s By-Laws Committee, where I refine the existing policies to improve student experiences. Together, these positions are an extension of my interest in public health, where I strive to contribute to the campus environment and well-being of the students.

Future Goals

As I continue at UNC, I hope to gain experience in a computational research laboratory. I am particularly interested in learning how researchers organize and analyze health-related datasets. Through this experience, I will have the opportunity to develop my skills in data analysis and learn to work with research datasets to investigate health issues. In the long term, I intend to pursue a career in Biostatistics. Ultimately, I want to contribute positively to the healthcare community through statistical and computational methods to investigate health-related questions.
